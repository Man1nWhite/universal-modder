# Business Woman — fixes after the first in-game look

Date: 2026-11-04. Follows `AUDIT-2026-11-04.md` (which is a measurement pass, not
a fix) and answers the four in-game screenshots: floating gloves, a bare gap
between cuff and glove, torn fabric on the upper arm / shoulder, and white
"wings" flaring over the shoulders.

Everything below is measured on the shipped model
(`build/skyrim-nif/business_woman.model`, rebuilt from `4.zip` + the 2B
template) and re-rendered offline in the pose that shows the defects
(`tools/pd2-las/pose_preview.py`, arms adducted). Two new checks were written for
this round: `tools/pd2-las/pose_zoom.py` (shoulder close-up) and
`tools/pd2-las/glove_check.py` (sleeve/glove junction with a glove proxy placed
on the PD2 hand bone).

## Root cause 1 — the glove was never reachable (fixed)

The 3BA source is authored to be worn *with hands*: every layer on the arm stops
short of the wrist so the wearer's own hand mesh covers the junction. PD2 does
not work that way — the heister's glove is spawned **at** the hand joint, so the
outfit has to reach it. Nothing in the pipeline did: `extend_blazer_sleeves`
stretched the blazer by 3 cm, which is 3 cm of the 6.3 cm that were missing, and
`tuck_shirt_cuffs` pulled the one layer that *did* reach further up the arm.

`build_models.extend_sleeves_to_wrist` redistributes the last 13 cm of each arm
layer along the forearm axis (rank-1 stretch: the cross-section, and therefore
the cuff radius, is untouched) until the layer reaches its target:

| layer | target (distance past the PD2 hand joint) | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| blazer (`jacket`) | −2.5 cm | ends deep inside the glove's cuff |
| bare arm (`skin`) | −2.2 cm | fills the sleeve mouth, stays 3 mm behind the cuff so no pale ring shows |
| shirt cuff (`jacket2`) | +1.0 cm | stays hidden under the blazer instead of ringing the glove |

Weights, UVs and topology are untouched, so the pose, the seam fix below and the
texture all survive. `extend_blazer_sleeves` and `tuck_shirt_cuffs` are no longer
called (kept in the module, docstrings marked superseded) — running them before
this pass fights it.

## Root cause 2 — the sleeve seam tears (fixed)

BodySlide exports each garment as a *sheet*: at every UV-island edge the row of
vertices is duplicated exactly, and the two rows share no edge. In Skyrim both
rows are skinned to the same bone, so nothing moves. The retarget gives each copy
its own influence list; where the copies disagree, the seam opens as soon as the
arm moves — that is the beige oval on the upper arm and the patches at the
shoulder blades in the screenshots.

`build_models.weld_open_seams` finds those coincident pairs *inside one material*
(0.2 mm tolerance, so a real opening such as the placket, the collar or the
sleeve mouth can never be paired) and averages their influence lists, normals and
positions. Measured on the shipped model: **3252 pairs** (skin 1275, blazer 826,
shirt 740, heels 362, skirt 32, necklace 17).

> This is *not* the seam weld that was rejected earlier (note in HANDOFF §5 /
> MODLOG: "welding the jacket's overlap edges shut distorts the sleeve"). That
> attempt merged the blazer's *overlap* edges, i.e. two different surfaces that
> happen to be close, which folds the sleeve. This pass only ever touches two
> rows that are the same point in space, and it moves no vertex by more than
> 0.1 mm — geometry cannot deform. The posed render shows no spike.

## Root cause 3 — the shirt rides out over the shoulder (mitigated)

Two separate things:

1. **Weights.** The shirt is 100 % arm-weighted across the shoulder while the
   blazer keeps a mixed `Spine2 / Shoulder / Arm` band. `build_models.follow_layer_weights`
   copies the nearest blazer vertex's influence list onto the shirt across the
   shoulder band (z 136–158 cm, 5900 vertices), so the two layers now deform
   together there and the shirt cannot emerge from under the jacket at any pose.
2. **Geometry.** The shirt's collar is as tall as the blazer's and its wing tips
   reach past the blazer's neckline. `build_models.tuck_shirt_collar` nips the
   shirt collar 7 % inward in x and 12 % down above z 150 cm (3997 vertices).
   The factors are deliberately mild: the white V down the chest is the outfit's
   design and stays as wide as it was.

Both maps are continuous at their threshold, so neither can tear the mesh.

# Round 2 — the bare shoulders (the real cause, and the fix)

Second screenshot batch: the gloves and the sleeve/glove junction are correct in
game, but **both shoulder tops are bare** (skin plus a white sliver, front and
back) with beige patches left on the upper arms. The zip the user tested was
`eac7d688…` (this tree's previous build), so this round had two jobs: find the
cause, and stop shipping a zip that is one build behind the model.

## The cause is the garment's neckline, not the weights

Everything that had been tried (welding the open seams, making the shirt ride the
blazer's weights, ~1 cm of shoulder room along the normals, tucking the underarm
skin, per-vertex inflation) attacks the same assumption — that the body pokes
*through* the jacket. It does not. A flat-colour ID render of the garment with
the body hidden shows the blazer's neck opening **flaring from the neck out to
the sleeve**: there is no fabric over the trapezius at all, front or back, and
the opening is identical in the bind pose and under adduction (0°/30°/50°), so
no weight or pose change can close it. Compared with the Skyrim source rendered
at the same scale and framing, the source's neckline meets the neck and the
blazer covers the shoulder from the collar to the sleeve.

The retarget gets the *body* right and the *garment* wrong: the source blazer's
collar/neckline vertices land ~5–8 cm out over the shoulder. In game that is the
band of skin across both shoulders.

## The fix: re-dress the garment on the shipped body

The one thing that survives the retarget exactly is the **correspondence**:
per-material vertex counts and triangle lists are identical (blazer 14 674
verts / 26 996 tris, shirt 10 385 / 18 455, skirt, heels, necklace — all equal
to the source), and `transfer_group` keeps the source order inside each material
slice. So for every garment vertex we know its source vertex, and the source
knows exactly how the garment sat on the body.

`bm.redress_from_source(group, source_model)` (called for the TPP body and the
shadow, right after the collar work and **before** the sleeve extension) rebuilds
each garment vertex as

```
built_body(j)  +  scale * ( clearance * n_built  +  R * tangential )
```

* `j` — the same body vertex (body order is preserved too), `n_built` its normal;
* `clearance` — signed distance from the source garment vertex to its nearest
  source body vertex along that body normal (k = 6, inverse-distance blended, so
  the field is smooth);
* `tangential` — the rest of the source offset, rotated by `R`, the minimal
  rotation from the source body normal to the built one;
* `scale` — the measured body spacing ratio (1.2574), i.e. the garment's
  clearances are carried over to a body that is a few percent larger.

Result on the shipped model (`[body] garment re-dressed from the source`):
25 059 verts (14 674 blazer + 10 385 shirt), mean 0.64 / 1.06 cm, peak 7.6 cm —
the peak is exactly the collar/lapel band that used to sit out on the shoulder.
Topology, UVs, weights and the signed distance field are untouched; only vertex
positions move, and `extend_sleeves_to_wrist` runs afterwards so the cuff still
reaches into the glove.

![source (top) vs shipped (bottom), shoulder band](previews/business_woman_shoulders.png)

Left/right shoulder band before → after (flat-colour render, % of the band that
is skin or shirt): 21.1 → 0.3 on the right band; the left band's number is a
framing artefact of the three-quarter view (it is a 22–23 % *designed* chest V
there) and is not used as a gate any more. The visual check is the gate: no skin
over either trapezius, front or back, with the body hidden or shown, at 0°, 30°
and 50° of arm adduction.

## What replaced what

* `give_shoulder_room` (1 cm push along the normals in the shoulder band, never
  committed) is **gone** — it moved 2 463 verts and changed ~64 px, because the
  fabric was not there to push. The re-dress supersedes it, `tuck_underarm_skin`
  and `tuck_shirt_sleeves` remain as small cleanups of the hidden skin.
* The zip is regenerated with the model this time:
  `dist/BusinessWoman_LAS.zip` sha256
  `18b5ac0306d3fc82b89b822125708fc1602a579d89849d2538c1f587352f9751`
  (the previous `eac7d688…` zip was built before the shoulder work, which is why
  the user kept seeing the old shoulders).

## Acceptance criterion, per the user

The user relaxed the "cover everything" reading: shirt and body skin showing
under the jacket is **fine**, what has to look right is the **chest, the
shoulders and the neck** — they must read as a well-dressed heister, not as a
collapsed or torn mesh. That is why the re-dress restores the *source's*
garment-to-body relationship rather than maximising coverage: the neckline ends
up where the outfit was designed to have it, the lapels sit on the chest, the
shoulder line is the source's shoulder line. Nothing below forces fabric over
skin that the design leaves open.

# Round 3 — the neck (and the chain that came with it)

The user: **"I see problems even in preview around the neck."** Correct — three
of them, and the numbers say why.

## 1. The neck was a stovepipe

The PD2 reference body shipped by the 2B pack is the ground truth for this rig:

| z (cm) | 2B reference body | this port's body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 154 | ±19.8 | ±14.9 |
| 157 | ±16.1 / ±15.3 | ±12.1 |
| 160 | **±4.7** | ±9.4 |
| 162 | ±4.8 | — |
| 163 | ±4.1 (top at 163.2) | ±7.3 |
| 166 | — | ±4.7 (top at 167.3) |

So above the trapezius the port carried an **18.8 cm wide** column where the rig
has a **9.4 cm** neck. That column is the 3BA *neck stub*: in Skyrim the head
mesh supplied most of the neck and the body stops at 157 cm. The retarget's axial
spine stretch lifts and keeps that stub wide, and no collar can close over it.

`bm.slim_neck` narrows only the column: a radial scale about the neck axis
(0, 7), full for r ≤ 8 cm and faded out by r = 14 cm so the trapezius and the
shoulder line are untouched, ramped in from z 157 to 161 so there is no step.
453 verts, peak 3.7 cm. Heights are deliberately **not** touched — the body's
neck still has to reach the PD2 head joint (166.8), and shortening it would open
a gap under the jaw in game.

## 2. The collar was flattened onto the shoulders

`lower_collar` (z 158, k 0.35) squashed everything above 158 toward it, which was
right when the collar was landing at 169 cm over a normal neck, but with the
re-dress the collar belongs at the top of the neck (~167 before the squash) and
the squash dragged it back down to 161 — a flat flange with 6 cm of bare neck
above it. `COLLAR_K` is now 0.55: the collar tops out at 163.3 / 164.3, i.e. a
standing collar on the neck, 3 cm below the head joint.

## 3. The necklace's chain loop

The chain is a closed loop around the neck. With a slimmer neck and a
higher collar it read as a **gold hoop standing on the neck**, and before the
neck work its loop was distorted by `lower_collar` into gold tabs over the
shoulders. Two changes:

* the necklace is now re-dressed with the garment (`mats=(jacket, jacket2,
  necklace)`), so it sits on the chest the way the source has it, and it is no
  longer in `lower_collar`'s squashed materials;
* `bm.tuck_necklace_chain` pulls the part of the loop above z 156 to 0.80 of its
  radius about the neck axis, i.e. *inside* the neck column, where the body
  hides it. Only the pendant and the chain in the open V stay visible — which is
  what the outfit looks like on the source body.

![neck, adducted 60°, textured](previews/business_woman_neck.png)

`tests/test_neck.py` pins both passes (narrows the column only, ramps in with
height, leaves other materials alone, sinks the loop only).

# Round 4 — why the neck went *through* the collar

Answer to "why the neck goes through collar in preview": **it was the Round 2
re-dress, and the reason is the Skyrim source has no body up there.**

`redress_from_source` places each garment vertex at the source's clearance from
the body. That is well defined only where the source *has* a body — and the
Skyrim mesh stops at **114.1 units (157 cm)**, handing the neck over to the head
mesh it does not ship. Every collar vertex above that height resolves to the
same handful of top-ring vertices, so the transferred "clearance" carries no
radial information: re-dressing them collapsed the shirt collar from a clean
stand collar into ragged, splayed flaps with spikes that cut straight through the
neck (isolated render of `mtr_jacket2` only: before = smooth folded band, after
= torn sheet).

Also, the neck itself was 2x too wide at those heights, so the collar's opening
was *inside* the neck (at z 162: neck 3.1-5.4 cm from the axis, shirt collar
1.8-6.0) — which is what made the two surfaces cross.

## Fixes

* `redress_from_source` now fades out where the source has no body:
  full effect below 108 source units, zero above 112 (`z_fade`). The collar keeps
  the retargeted shape it had; everything the source body actually backs (the
  chest, the neckline, the shoulders, the V) is still re-dressed.
* `slim_neck` (Round 3) stays, so the collar's opening now clears the neck.
* `tuck_necklace_chain` additionally clamps the chain's radius to 0.75x the neck
  radius at that height — a flat 0.8x scale still left the top of the loop
  outside the narrow upper neck, which is what the gold hoop was.

Dead ends, measured and removed: a per-vertex outward push along the body normal
(collared the neck correctly but made the rim jagged, one vertex at a time) and a
height-coherent radial push of the whole collar band (fixed the intersection,
destroyed the lapels and the collar's shape — the blazer's neck opening and its
front V are one boundary loop, and the chest panel shares the height band). A
rim-only push along the boundary loops moved just 78 verts and still flared the
collar, because the shape damage was already in the sheet above 157, not at the
rim.
